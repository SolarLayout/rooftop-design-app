The table stands under the options and is of the chosen option (decision 17): a row for each MPPT that has strings, from the engine's mppt_loads. A puts 4, 4, 4, 4, 3 and 3 strings on the six MPPTs of INV-1; four strings draw Imp 54.3 A and Isc 57.2 A of the 65.0 A an MPPT takes; kWp is the strings × 29 × 560 W, 64.96 for four.

The row is the table of the kit at the inspector's width: the MPPT with its inverter under it, the strings, Imp and Isc in one column of two lines, each of its limit, the kWp and the mark. A tick where both are within the limit; over it the row has a note and the verdict names the MPPT (decision 18).

Without a current limit in the OND every limit is not known: the row says “of not known” and has a note mark, never a red cross. Under the table, the API's words (max_current_per_mppt.note) and a link to the field of the current per MPPT. The options note says the same: “The OND gives no current limit per MPPT: one string for each of its 28 inputs at most, 4 per MPPT, so one inverter takes 24 strings.”

Each field is empty while the engine's default holds, and says the default and its source beside it (decision 7): the lowest and the highest ambient from the weather, 11.9 and 39.3 °C; the cell rise by roof type, 25 °C on the flat roof with open racks. A value entered stays, even when it equals the default; “Use the weather's” sets it back to none.

The Voc coefficient is the one-diode model's, −0.164 %/°C, the demo's PAN stating none; the module's note says so in the engine's words, with true minus signs. Entered, it sizes the strings (decision 6): Voc at the lowest ambient is Voc at STC × (1 + μ × (t_min − 25)).

With −0.29 entered the field says “entered” and the note goes. The run sizes the strings with it: Voc cold 51.84 V a module against 51.04 V, so at most 28 fit under 1500 V (1,452 V for 28) and the range is 14 to 28. The options of that run are not in the data of this board; the marker and the verdict, which follow the option it chooses, are left out.
