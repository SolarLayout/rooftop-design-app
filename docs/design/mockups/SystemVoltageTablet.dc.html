System voltage: set by the user's country

Today a new design waits for a system voltage: the Strings lens opens on a decision card and nothing is sized until one is chosen. Proposed (D87): a new design starts with the voltage of the user's country, 600 V in the United States and Canada (the limit for homes: NEC 690.7, CE Code Section 64), 1000 V everywhere else, and India when the country cannot be found. The first run brings strings, options and energy. The choice stays the user's: the same three, one press away. Shown on the Pune demo with the country given to the prototype.

The app caught in the browser, with a prototype of the Strings lens; for approval.

Where it comes from

Beside the heading, “Set for India” (the app) or “Your choice” (the user). Under the choice, one line: “1000 V, the usual limit for rooftops in India. Change it if the site needs another.”; in the United States “600 V, the limit for homes in the United States (NEC 690.7). Change it if the site allows more.”

The country

From the request (Vercel's country), else the browser's time zone, else India. Nothing is asked and nothing is kept but the voltage and who set it.

Set by the app, equipment rated under it

The app lowers its own value to the highest of 600, 1000, 1500 V at or under the rating, so the screens and the bill of materials say what is built: “600 V, to match this inverter, which takes up to 800 V. 1000 V is the default in India.” No edit counted.

Your choice, equipment rated under it

Never moved. A note with the exclamation mark: “Strings sized to 1100 V, this inverter's limit. Your system is set to 1500 V.” and Use 1000 V, an edit like any other, undone in one step.

Equipment rated over it

Never raised by the app, whoever set it: the value may be the legal ceiling of the place. One line and a button: “This inverter takes up to 1500 V. Raise it only if the site's code allows.” Use 1500 V. Where no string length fits, the line says which voltage would (the United States board).

The picker

An inverter rated under the voltage set carries the exclamation mark and its reason under its maker: “Up to 800 V: strings sized under the 1000 V set”. Nothing is hidden; the rest carry nothing.

What leaves a new design

The decision card “Choose a system voltage”, the list of what waits for it, and the vitals' waiting state. A project without a voltage (a call to the API) still waits and says so; a design of before gets the default when it is opened.

Help

The topic “System voltage” says how the default is set, with the references, and drops “Nothing is chosen for you”.
