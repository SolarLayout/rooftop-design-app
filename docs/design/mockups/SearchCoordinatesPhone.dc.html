Coordinates in the search

Coordinates copied from Google Earth, in degrees, minutes and seconds, are read as decimal ones are. What cannot be read says why, and what to do. Phone 390.

Pasted or typed: read back before it goes

As soon as what is in the field reads as coordinates, one row under it says where it lands, marked as the first place found is. Enter, Find my roof or a press on the row goes there. Nothing is asked of the address search.

When it cannot be read: it says why, and what to do

What looks like coordinates is never sent to the address search. The note comes when Enter or Find my roof is pressed, not while typing. The mark is the note’s exclamation, never a cross.

Minutes or seconds of 60 or more
  • Minutes and seconds go up to 59. The latitude has 65″: check where it was copied from.
A latitude past 90°
  • A latitude goes up to 90°, and this one is 95°. Check where it was copied from.
The longitude first, without letters: what it may mean is offered
  • The latitude comes first, and 151.21530 is more than 90°. Did you mean this?
  • 33.85678° S, 151.21530° ESwapped
Two of a kind
  • Both are marked N. The latitude takes N or S, the longitude E or W.
One alone
  • Only the latitude is here. Add the longitude after it: 12°54′50.74″N 77°29′31.16″E.
A minus and a letter that disagree
  • −12.91409 is marked N, and a minus means south. Keep the minus or the letter, not both.
Part of it cannot be read
  • These look like coordinates, but the longitude cannot be read. Write them as 12°54′50.74″N 77°29′31.16″E, or 12.91409, 77.49199.
The address search not available: plain words, never the service’s own (“Invalid Token”)
  • The address search is not available right now. Paste coordinates or a maps link instead.
