Stage, Layers

The Layers button of the view controls, beside Fit and the north button, in any lens: an icon button that opens a small panel with one switch, Satellite imagery, on by default. Off, the ground under the building is the plain grid and the attribution goes with the imagery. Desktop whole; the button's states; the switch off; tablet and phone in parts.

The screen

Above, the Studio on desktop 1440 by 900 in the Array lens: the approved board with the Layers button after the north button, open, and its panel; Satellite imagery on, as it is at first for a project with a location; the imagery under the building, the attribution at the stage's bottom left under the vitals, 24 from the left and 6 from the bottom, as the stage shows it since Phase 5.

The imagery is the illustration of the Find boards laid on the ground of the 3D by the same projection; the product shows Esri World Imagery, stitched about the roof's centroid (Ground.tsx). The vignette of the stage stays over it, so that the picture fades into the ground at the edges, as the 3D of the boards has always faded.

The words: the button is “Layers”; the panel's title “Layers”; the switch “Satellite imagery” with the hint “Off: the plain grid”. Sentence case, plain words, no new term: the information architecture's own (“satellite imagery”, “a plain dark grid”).

The button and its states

Rest: control, the line, shadow-raised, the icon in text-2
Hover: control-hover, the line-hover, raised by 1, shadow-raised-hover, the icon in text
Focus: the ring of 2 in accent, 2 from the button
Open: control-pressed, the line-strong, shadow-pressed, the icon in text; aria-expanded

The Layers button is the fourth control of the view, after Fit and the north button, 8 from it: a secondary icon button of 38 (44 on tablet and phone), control with the line, radius-control (radius-row on touch), shadow-raised, as the north button beside it; the icon is three stacked sheets, 20 (22 on touch), stroke 2, in text-2. Its name is “Layers”; it has no word on the stage, as the north button has none.

Its states are the icon button's (components.md, “Buttons”): hover raised by 1 with shadow-raised-hover and the icon in text; pressed lowered by 1; the focus ring; open, it looks pressed and stays so while the panel is open (aria-expanded), as the trigger of a Select does. It has no disabled state: the switch inside can always be turned. The button looks the same whether the imagery is on or off: the ground under the roof says which.

In any lens: the button is of the stage and comes with it, as the view controls and the time control do (components.md, “Top bar and navigation”). What it sets is of the view, not of the design, kept in the view store: a project file carries no ground.

The panel is the FloatingPanel of the Select and the project switcher at the width of a card's menu, 240: surface-1, border line-strong, radius-card-s, shadow-float, padding 6; it stands 6 under the button with their right edges in line, so that it stays on the stage (the panel of a Select has its left edge on its trigger's; here the trigger is at the stage's right edge, and place-floating keeps the panel on the screen).

Its title, “Layers”, is a section label (text-section, text-3). Its one row is the switch of components.md beside its label, “Satellite imagery”, in body text, and one line of hint in text-caption text-3, “Off: the plain grid”: what the other position gives, as the switches of the inspector say it (“Off: positions shaded inside the window stay empty”). Nothing else is in it; a second layer, when there is one, is a second row.

Open, the focus is on the switch; the space bar turns it. Escape, a press on the button and a press beside the panel close it, and the focus goes back to the button. The switch takes effect at once: the ground changes under the roof while the panel is open, so that the choice is seen. The panel's motion is duration-panel; under reduced motion it cuts.

On tablet and phone the panel stays a floating panel of the stage, not a bottom sheet: it is one switch, and the ground it changes must stay in sight while it is turned; a sheet would cover the inspector's sheet on phone and the vitals on tablet. The row is 48 high and the switch takes the touch sizes of the kit, 52 by 30 in a hit area of 52 by 44.

The switch off: the ground under the building is the plain ground of the 3D palette, groundPlain with a gridLine every 10 m, as the stage draws it before the imagery's tiles come and where the project has no location (Ground.tsx), and as the approved Studio boards draw it. The building, its shadow on the ground and the vignette of the stage stay as they are; the imagery alone goes.

The attribution goes with the imagery: it stands under the 3D only while the imagery is shown (guidelines.md, “3D scene”: satellite imagery carries its attribution wherever it is shown). Off, the stage's bottom left is empty.

Where the project has no location, or there is no map key, the ground is the plain grid whatever the switch says: the switch stays on and the panel says nothing more, since the state “Offline or no map key” is said where the flow continues (guidelines.md, “States every screen must handle”). The ground that is drawn is said to a screen reader in the stage's text equivalent, as it is today (“imagery” or “plain”).
