`Day` in the Sun view chooses the day the view shows. `Design day`, in the Array step's Spacing, is the day the rows are spaced for.

## What to set

Keep the winter solstice, the shortest day: the sun stands lowest and the shadows are longest, so rows spaced for it stand furthest apart. It is set in the Array step, under Spacing: `Design day` offers the winter solstice of the roof's side of the equator (`21 Dec` in the north, `21 Jun` in the south), `21 Jun` and `21 Mar`. Its hint says `The day the rows are spaced for. 21 Dec has the longest shadows.`

## What it changes

With the <HelpLink k="studio.section.shade-free-hours">shade-free hours</HelpLink>, the design day sets the row spacing on tilted rows, the gap between east-west units, and the area round obstacles and the parapet kept free of modules. The app lays out the roof again, then runs the design.

## In the Sun view

`Day` chooses the day whose sun, path and shadows the view shows. It changes what is seen, not the design. `21 Dec`, `21 Mar` and `21 Jun` are the solstices and the March equinox; `Any date` opens a `Day` field and a `Month` list. The line under it names the design day, as `Design day: 21 December, the winter solstice in this hemisphere`. Choose the design day's date and the view follows the design day, even when it changes. The `[` and `]` keys step a day back or forward.

## When to change it

When the client takes some winter shade between the rows for more modules: a day nearer summer gives shorter shadows, so the rows stand closer.
