## What to set

Keep the voltage the app set from your country, unless the site's code allows another: `600 V` in the United States, its territories and Canada, `1000 V` everywhere else. The country is the one your connection comes from, else your device's time zone, else India.

The words beside the heading say who set it: `Set for` your country, `Default` for the app's value of another country, or `Yours` once you choose. The line under the choice says where the app's value comes from.

## What it changes

It caps how many modules one string holds. On the coldest morning a string's Voc, its voltage with no current flowing, is at its highest. It must stay at or under the lowest of three limits: the system voltage, the inverter's highest DC voltage and the module's rating. A higher system voltage allows longer strings, and fewer of them. The design runs again when you choose, and the bill of materials names the DC cable by the voltage set.

## When to change it

When the site's code allows a higher voltage, or asks for a lower one. The app never raises it. When the inverter and the module take more, or when no string length fits and a higher voltage would fit one, the step offers it with a button such as `Use 1500 V`. Press it only where the site's code allows.

The equipment never moves it either. When the inverter or the module is rated under it, the strings are sized to that rating and the step says so. If you chose the voltage, a note with an exclamation mark says so, with a button for the voltage that matches. A module file that gives no rating is taken at the app's value, and the step says so.
