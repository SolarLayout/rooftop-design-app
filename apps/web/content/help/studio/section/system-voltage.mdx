## How it limits a string

A string's open-circuit voltage is highest when its modules are coldest. At the coldest sizing temperature, the voltage of the whole string must stay at or under the lowest of three limits: the system voltage, the inverter's highest DC input voltage, and the module's highest system voltage from its file (`1000 V` when the file gives none). That sets the most modules a string can hold.

## Where it starts

A new design starts with the voltage of your country, so that it is sized and run at once. In the United States and its territories, and in Canada, it is `600 V`, the limit for homes there (NEC 690.7; CE Code, Section 64). Everywhere else it is `1000 V`. The country is the one your connection comes from, else your device's time zone, else India. The words beside the heading say who set it: `Set for` your country (`Set by the app` for a design made where the default differs), or `Your choice` once you choose. The line under the choice says where the app's value comes from.

It is yours to change: the right voltage depends on the site, its building and the code that applies there.

## When the equipment is rated lower

The voltage is never moved for you. When the inverter or the module is rated under it, the strings are sized to that rating, and the lens says so. For a voltage you chose, it says so with an exclamation mark, and with a button for the highest of the three at or under the rating, where there is one. A module whose file gives no rating is taken as `1000 V`, and the lens says that the limit is taken, not the module's.

## Going higher

The app never raises the voltage. When both the inverter and the module take more, or when no string length fits and a higher voltage would fit one, the lens offers it with a button: never above the lower of their two ratings. Choose it only where the site's code allows it.

<Callout>The bill of materials names the DC cable by the voltage set.</Callout>
