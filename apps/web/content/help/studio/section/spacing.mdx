## What it says

The section opens with one sentence: how far apart the rows stand, and the hours and the day they keep each other free of shade, as `Rows stand 6.45 m apart so that they do not shade each other from 09:00 to 16:00 on 21 Dec.` Then its fields: `Row spacing from the shade-free hours`, `Shade-free hours`, `Design day`, `Direction (azimuth)` and `Allow shaded positions`. On `Flat, east-west` there is no row spacing; a `Sloped, flush` roof has no spacing nor direction: its rows follow one another up the roof and face the way the roof slopes.

## Row spacing

The distance on plan from the front edge of one row to the front edge of the next. With `Row spacing from the shade-free hours` on, the engine works it out from the <HelpLink k="studio.section.shade-free-window">shade-free hours</HelpLink> and rounds it up slightly, from the sun's lowest profile angle in those hours (the ground coverage ratio, GCR, follows from it). Switched off, the field starts at the last spacing and takes your own, and the sentence says what shade-free would be.

## Direction (azimuth)

The direction the rows face, in degrees clockwise from north. With `Follow the roof edge` on, the rows face square off the roof edge closest to the equator; on an east-west roof the ridges run square to that edge, each unit's two faces turned east and west of it. Switched off, the field starts at the azimuth the layout used, and the line under it says where that edge faces.

## How it behaves

Switching back on hands the value back to the engine. Each change lays the roof out again, and the design runs again.

Closed, the section is one line: how far apart the rows stand and the shade-free hours, with `Default` while the app's values hold and `Yours` once you give one.
