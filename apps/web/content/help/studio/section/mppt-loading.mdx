## Reading it

An MPPT is an input of the inverter that tracks a group of strings at their best working point. Closed, the row lists the strings on each MPPT of the chosen option, and says `within limits`, `over a limit` or `a limit not known`.

Open it for a row for each MPPT that has strings, named with its inverter: the strings on it, its current against its limit, its kWp, and a mark. Imp is the working current and Isc the short-circuit current, the current with the string's ends joined. The mark is `Fits`, `Over the limit` or `Limit not known`. On a phone the rows are cards.

## How it is worked out

The app shares the option's strings among its inverters in layout order, then spreads them over each inverter's MPPTs as evenly as they go. An MPPT's Imp is its strings times the module's Imp, its Isc its strings times the module's Isc, and its kWp its strings times the modules per string times the module's power.

Imp is held to the current per MPPT of the inverter file, or else the inverter's DC input current shared among its MPPTs. Isc is held to the file's short-circuit current per MPPT, or else the same current. The app's options put no more strings on an MPPT than both limits allow, at least one; your own option is not held to them.

## When a limit is not known

The note under the table says so. Press `Enter the current per MPPT`: it opens `Inverter limits and cabling` with the cursor in `Current per MPPT`. Type the datasheet's value. It replaces both limits, and the design runs again. Until then the option carries an exclamation mark.
