## What a card shows

Its letter and why it is there, then modules in series × strings and the power in kWp (with the inverters when more than one), then the energy of the first year in MWh. The words say what sets it apart: `Recommended`, `Your own`, `Fills the inverter`, `Less clipping`, a miss of the DC/AC target such as `Below target`, or else `Option` and its letter. A tick means it is in its DC/AC target and every MPPT is within its current limit; an exclamation mark means one of those is not so. The options come best first; those that miss the DC/AC target fold under one line, `2 more, below the DC/AC target`, which opens them.

## The chosen one

The chosen card says that its length fits, `29 modules a string fits at 1500 V`, and `Why` opens the proof: the lengths that fit at the system voltage, the most that fit and the fewest, then the coldest morning (the string's open-circuit voltage against the lowest of the system, module and inverter ratings) and the hottest afternoon (its MPP voltage against the inverter's MPPT minimum). For an option that misses something, the line carries an exclamation mark instead of a tick, and the proof adds what the engine says it misses.

## How the run builds them

The engine tries up to three lengths from the series range: the one nearest the inverter's nominal MPP voltage, the longest, and the one from that nominal length up that places the most modules on the roof, or one shorter than the longest when that is one of the other two. For each it forms strings along the rows, then wires what the rows leave at their ends into strings that run on into the next rows, so the roof's positions are used. It ranks the strings in a row first and those across rows after them, each least shaded first. It then makes up to three combinations of strings and inverters: the most strings that keep DC/AC in target, adding inverters when the target needs them, the strings that fill those inverters, and every string the roof holds. Each is simulated for a year. Options in target come first, then by energy; at most five are kept, and the first is `Recommended` only when it is in target. `How the options are made` opens three numbered lines: the DC/AC target, how many strings one MPPT takes and the current they carry, and that the least shaded strings are used first, with a line for the options that miss the target.

## Choosing one

A press on a card, or an arrow key, chooses it at once: every option is in the run's answer already, so nothing runs again. The chosen option sets the marker on the <HelpLink k="studio.section.modules-in-series">Modules in series</HelpLink> bar and the <HelpLink k="studio.section.mppt-loading">MPPT loading</HelpLink>.

## Your own

`Add your own option` stands under the options and opens `Modules a string`, `Strings` and `Inverters`, each held to what fits: a length in the range, as many strings of it as the roof holds, and no more inverters than strings. Before you add it, the form says whether it fits and gives its power and DC/AC ratio. `Add option` saves it and chooses it, and the design runs again; `Change` and `Remove` edit it later. When the engine cannot build it, its card says why. While the strings are sized for the first time, three blocks stand where the cards will, and the step says `Sizing the strings`. When no length fits, the problem at the head of the step says so and this section shows no cards; when the roof holds too few positions for a string, this section says so instead of showing cards. When the roof is too small for the run's options yet holds a string, `Add your own option` stands under the words, its `Modules a string` set to that string's length.
