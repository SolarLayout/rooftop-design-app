## The four tiles

The card `Shade over the year` on the stage of the Sun view (on a phone or a tablet, the work column) says the energy lost to shade, by its cause.

- `Rows and obstacles`: the light lost to shadows on the roof, from the rows in front and from the parapet and the obstacles. It counts the shaded part of the direct light and the part of the sky they hide from each module, as a share of the light that gets past the horizon.
- `Strings`: the energy lost on top of that because a module's bypass diodes and cell layout turn a partial shadow into a larger loss, and a string runs at its weakest module. It needs strings, so it waits, and says why, until there is an option.
- `Horizon`: the light the distant horizon takes: the direct light while the sun is behind it, and the part of the sky it hides, as a share of the light with no horizon at all. The horizon hides the same part of the sky from every module.
- `Area kept clear`: the area inside the setback that the shadows of the parapet and the obstacles cover through the shade-free hours of the design day. The layout leaves it empty unless shaded positions are allowed.

The card's close button folds it to a chip at its place, `Shade over the year` with the three losses added, which opens it again. The sun's arc and the timeline stay. Open or folded is remembered on this device.

## What the figures count

The line under the tiles says which modules are counted. With a chosen option, the figures are its own, from its energy losses, for the modules it uses. Before there is an option, near and far shading count every position of the layout. A second line says whether the weather source gave a horizon profile; without one, far shading is nothing. PVGIS gives one with its weather; when it sent none for the site, the line says so, and Performance notes it under its Weather assumption.
