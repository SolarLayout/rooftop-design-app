## Reading it

A string is a run of modules wired one after another, in series. The app sizes its length from the system voltage, the inverter, and the coldest and hottest hours of the site.

Under the chosen option, one line says how many modules per string it uses and whether that length fits at the system voltage, with a tick. `Why` opens the proof:

- the lengths that fit, from the fewest to the most, with a marker at the chosen length;
- the coldest morning: the string's Voc, its voltage with no current flowing, against the lowest limit;
- the hottest afternoon: the string's working voltage against the inverter's MPPT minimum.

An option that misses its DC/AC target or an MPPT's current limit has an exclamation mark on the line instead, and the proof says what it misses.

## What sets the ends

When there are no options, the `Modules per string` bar stands in their place and names what sets each end.

- The fewest, `MPPT minimum`: on the hottest afternoon, the highest ambient plus the cell rise, the string's working voltage must stay at or over the inverter's MPPT minimum.
- The most, `Voc limit`: on the coldest morning, at the lowest ambient, the string's Voc must stay at or under the lowest of the system voltage, the inverter's highest DC voltage and the module's rating.
- The most, `MPPT maximum`: on the coldest morning, the string's working voltage must stay at or under the inverter's MPPT maximum. The tighter of the two sets the end.

The temperatures and the Voc coefficient are under `Sizing temperatures`. `Lowest ambient` and `Highest ambient` come from the weather, `Cell rise` from the roof type, and the Voc coefficient from the module file until you type the datasheet's.

## When no length fits

When the fewest modules the inverter needs are more than a string can hold, there are no options, your own included. The step says `No string length fits at` the voltage at its head, and the `Modules per string` bar says `None fit`. Its buttons are the ways out: `Choose another inverter`, `Change the sizing temperatures` and, where a higher voltage the equipment takes would fit a string, a button such as `Use 1500 V`. Press that one only where the site's code allows.
