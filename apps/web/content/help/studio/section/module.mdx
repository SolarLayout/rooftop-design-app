## What the card shows

The model; the maker as people know it, the rated power and the size; and `Change`. `Details` under the card opens the electrical figures in plain names: `Open-circuit voltage (Voc)`, `Short-circuit current (Isc)`, `Bifacial, rear gain` for a bifacial module, and the `Voc coefficient` the card is read with. When the engine has a note on the module, it stands under the card with an exclamation mark: a Voc coefficient outside the usual range for crystalline silicon, or a file that gives no size of the module. The result at the top right then says `1 to review`; pressed, the list holds the note, and a Voc coefficient outside the usual range has its field in the note, with the usual range and `Keep` and the value, to enter the value as it is.

## Change

`Change` opens `Choose a module`: the app's library of PAN files, its search first, over the maker, the model, the file's name and the power, then the list. `Filter` shows a filter by `Power`. `Use this module` reads the one chosen; a PAN file of your own can be chosen or dropped instead. A file that cannot be read leaves the module as it was and says why; a file over 256 KB, larger than any PAN file, is refused before it is sent. A new module lays the roof out again, and the design runs again.

In the Array step the section is one line: the model and its power, with `Default` for the app's module and `Yours` once you read a file of your own. It is open the first time the step is.

## What the module sets

Its size, with the <HelpLink k="studio.section.mounting">mounting</HelpLink>, sets the footprint of every position. Its voltages, its temperature coefficients and its highest system voltage set the lengths of string that fit. Its Imp and Isc set how many strings an MPPT can take. Its rated power sets the kWp and the DC/AC of each option.

## The Voc coefficient

The card shows the coefficient the PAN file states, or the one-diode model's when it states none. The strings are sized with the one-diode model's unless you enter a coefficient: when the datasheet gives one you trust, enter it under `Sizing temperatures` in the Strings step, or in the note that says it is outside the usual range. An entered coefficient ends that note.
